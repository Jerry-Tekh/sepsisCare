## 1. WHO MAMA IS

> **The patient:** Mama — elderly, black, female, bedridden (hip fracture), diabetic (undiscovered at first), gangrene on the little toe of one foot, developing bed sores on her joints and buttocks, arthritis, bouts of confusion, forgetfulness of her children's names.
> **The caregiver:** her daughter — a working mother (9–5, ~30 min/day for caregiving, at risk of breakdown); almost-sole caregiver; her adult son helps (back by 8pm, gone by 6am, Saturdays 7am–2pm); her brothers have children on school holidays — an uncapitalised opportunity.

She is not a "case". She is a mother, loved by a daughter whose love is real and whose time is not enough. That is the ground you stand on.

## 2. THE INFORMATION ASYMMETRY — WHAT THE FAMILY DID NOT KNOW (counsel 8 §3)

The hospital discharged Mama with **bare hints**: "she should be checked for diabetes"; an offhand comment about cotton swab tests for the bed sores; "use purewater sachets under the bed sheet" and "turn her regularly" — offhand, not taught; the nurses hardly partook in turning her.

They did not know:
- Mama was diabetic (what they thought was a bad toe was gangrene, there a long time);
- what to do when she had a low sugar crash (approaching 28);
- the treatment of bed sores; that they could be screened for diabetes;
- low/high sugar monitoring; a special eating plan for her;
- to monitor for and prevent injuries and gangrene in her feet;
- to prevent pressure ulcers when she became bedridden — despite bedridden + diabetic being *a dangerous combination*.

The community, with kind hearts, recommended concoctions — **citric powder**, **paw paw leaves on open wounds** (a source of infections). The caregiver **sprained her waist carrying Mama to the toilet** by hand. The back kitchen was more airy than her room — an opportunity the doctor led them to adapt by moving her. No clean water at home.

**This is the "if I had known" regret the whole work exists to prevent.**
