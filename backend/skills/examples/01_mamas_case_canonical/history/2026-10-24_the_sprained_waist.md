# PATIENT-SET HISTORY — 2026-10-24 — THE SPRAINED WAIST AND THE SAFE TRANSFER

> Counsel 4 §3: the record of issues particularly important — the issue, its date, what it turned out to be; anything useful for diagnosis by a doctor or for inference later.

**The issue:** The caregiver sprained her waist **carrying Mama to the toilet** — they carried her by hand, unsupervised, the only transfer method they knew.

**What it turned out to be:** Found during the doctor's one-week home stay (counsel 10 §3.3 — the doctor observes the actual execution, what conversation cannot see). Not an accident to shrug off: a **manual-handling risk flag** (counsel 1 §6) — the exact precursor pattern of caregiver injury → patient neglect → missed wound care.

**What was done:**
- Safe transfer taught, in person, by the doctor; "never carry alone" made a standing rule in every plan.
- **Wheelchair recommended** — doubly: it protects her waist AND moves Mama to fresher air easily, keeping her psychologically active among people (counsel 23 §9's exact reasoning).
- Incontinence-management guidance added (diaper change cadence — UTI prevention).
- Protection flag recorded in `caregiver_experience_space`; strategy s3 updated with respect of s1 (the turning schedule re-planned around her healing waist — lighter duties weekdays).

**Useful for diagnosis/inference later:** recurring waist pain in a caregiver of a bedridden patient is a *system* signal, not a personal one — look for the transfer method first, before ever naming non-compliance.
