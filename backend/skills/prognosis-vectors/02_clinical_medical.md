## 3. FRUIT 1 — EDUCATING TIMELY CONCERNING COMPLICATION PREVENTION: THE THREE TRACKS (counsel 23 §5.2)

**The three tracks are the default triage** (counsel 23 §10): when you encounter a patient-set state, you first determine which track it is in — this decides the education and the protocols.

### Track 1 — Primary Prevention (Root Causes & Intermediate States)
- **Goal:** prevent the formation of portals of entry.
- **Target patient:** has root causes (e.g., hidden diabetes, heart failure/edema) or intermediate states (e.g., prolonged immobility, neuropathy) but **no open wounds or active infections**.
- **System action:** generate microlearning on proactive care — daily foot inspections, elevating legs to reduce edema, turning schedules to prevent pressure ulcers, dietary management for diabetes.
- **The Mama case:** she is bedridden with diabetes — no open wound on the feet yet → Track 1: the foot inspections, the turning schedules, the diet.

### Track 2 — Secondary Prevention (Portals of Entry)
- **Goal:** prevent the infection of existing portals of entry and stop local infections from progressing to systemic sepsis.
- **Target patient:** has an active portal of entry (surgical wound, diabetic foot ulcer, pressure sore, catheter site).
- **System action:** strict, step-by-step protocols for sterile technique — 2-minute surgical handwashing, proper dressing changes, recognizing local signs of infection (heat/purulence).
- **The Mama case:** bed sores present on joints and buttocks; gangrene on the little toe → Track 2: the sterile-dressing protocols, the infection signs.

### Track 3 — Combined Prevention (The Most Common Reality)
- **Goal:** manage existing wounds while preventing new ones.
- **Target patient:** has an active wound (Secondary) AND underlying conditions like diabetes or immobility (Primary).
- **System action:** interleave both protocols, **carefully managing caregiver fatigue so they are not overwhelmed by the dual workload**.
- **The Mama case:** the bed sores (Secondary) AND the diabetes + immobility (Primary) → Track 3: both protocols interleaved, the fatigue managed.

**Cross-reference:** the three tracks are the concrete system of counsel 11's timely-guidance counsel — each track is "on time" for a different stage of prognosis (Workflow 14 §3).

## 5. THE ROLES THE PATIENT-SET PLAYS (counsel 23 §3 + §9 — Mama's case)

1. **Being led by God to achieve progress in prognosis** — e.g., preventing bed sores from appearing.
2. **Creating new opportunities** — e.g., getting a car/means of transport for emergency.
3. **Mitigating detrimental components** — by two moves:
   - **Converting them into opportunities** — e.g., replacing the hard bed (detrimental within the context of a bedridden patient) with a water bed, ensuring appropriate filling/pressure, and so stepping better in preventing bed sores.
   - **Removing their influence** — e.g., getting mosquito netting for a mosquito-infested environment, and so reducing malaria risk.

**The medical-space work (counsel 23 §9 — "what to do to progress the recovery of the patient"):**
- **Manage existing portals of entry** (bed sores, gangrene) to prevent infections and other complications — prevent infection of bed sores and heal them; to prevent infection, use sterile dressing processes such as 2-min handwashing.
- **Manage risk factors to avoid complications** — diabetes (eating well, regular check for foot sores), arthritis (moving joints well).
- **Prevent new portals of entry** — bed sores, injuries (using warm water and not hot; avoid dragging them over the bed to prevent injuries).
- **Account for host status** (file 03) — managing old age determines the food that should be taken and constrains her diabetes-diet.
- **Account for reported bouts of confusion** — Mama puts clothes on her head, wears clothes in a tangled manner (the fishing — Workflow 4; the stringing — the UTI likelihood).

## 9. PITFALLS

1. **The track-confusion failure** — educating with the wrong track (the sterile protocols for a patient with no portal of entry; the proactive care for a patient with an active infection). Determine the track first — it decides the education.
2. **The fatigue-forgotten failure** — interleaving the protocols without managing the caregiver fatigue (counsel 23 §5.2 Track 3's exact warning). The dual workload is real — manage it.
3. **The generic-protocol failure** — giving protocols not grounded in the matched guide or the doctor's instructions. Ground or ask (Workflow 12).
4. **The diagnose-creep failure** — diagnosing ("that is a diabetic ulcer") — the doctor's role. You refer, you flag, you escalate — never diagnose.
5. **The unnoticed-deterioration failure** — not noting the stage when it changes (the red marks → the high-risk stage). Stage-note immediately (Workflow 14 §4).
6. **The once-managed-always-managed failure** — managing the condition once and never re-noting it. The vector is living — re-noted as it changes.
7. **The prescription-boundary failure** — advising on medication changes. Route to the doctor (Workflow 15's out-of-scope meekness).
