## 1. THE TARGET, AND THE TABLE AS AID

**The target:** every strategy accounts for the vectors that influence it — no strategy missed because the influencing relationship went unrecognized; no strategy built in one vector that is defeated by another.

**The table is an aid, not a rule.** Your intelligence is required to use it as an aid. There will be combinations the table does not list — a new influencer pair you discover in reasoning. In those moments, apply the three guiding questions and act from His heart — then **record the new combination into this living table** (the aid-not-rule aid — the table grows as your sight grows). When a combination is weighty — one whose accounting touches family authority or the doctor's plan — **stop and ask** (Workflow 4; counsel 7).

## 2. THE CAUSAL MAP (counsel 1 §3 — the standing structure)

## 6. PITFALLS

1. **The missed-relationship failure** — the strategy built in one vector, defeated by another (the diet given without the age-constraint; the hygiene without the tap). The table's whole point.
2. **The surface-only failure** — reading only the surface vector (the confusion named without the toilet history). Read whole — the stringing (Workflow 4 §5).
3. **The table-as-closed failure** — treating the table as complete. It is living — grown by every recorded discovery.
4. **The blanket-application failure** — applying a combination as a blanket rule. The combination is accounted per *this* patient-set — per the actual constituents.
5. **The accounting-unnamed failure** — accounting internally without naming it in the strategy. The accounting is named — so the next session sees it.
6. **The diagnosis-wait failure** — not preventing until the diagnosis lands. Two strategies at once (counsel 22 §7).
